# Constellation co-op pilot — planet-1, 2026-08-11

Two pi sessions playtested Constellation like a couch pair: **Laptop**
(`gemini-3.5-flash-lite`) drove the astronaut through the game driver; **Phone**
(`gemini-3.1-flash-lite`) held the spellbook through the phone driver; they
coordinated over the intercom extension (channel `playtest-20260811-123800`,
33 messages). Run: ~5 minutes, **25 deaths, no clear**, both seats wrote their
reports unprompted and signed off. Raw per-seat reports are appended verbatim;
screenshots and the full message log are in `reports/` and
`.pi/intercom/playtest-20260811-123800/` on the machine that ran it.

## Read this first — what the pilot is and isn't

- The phone driver executed all puzzle *mechanics* (mandatory: QuickMath/Trivia
  give 30s for 3 items; a free-tier LLM turn costs ~12s+). The players owned
  strategy, timing, and coordination. Puzzle-content observations below are
  from driver transcripts, labeled as such.
- LLM players coordinate at 5–15s per exchange; couch humans talk in ~1s. The
  pacing findings are therefore *amplified* — but directionally real: they're
  what any latency (distraction, phone fumbling, a toddler) does to this game.
- Feasibility is not in question: the scripted rails run (`verify-rails.sh`)
  clears planet-1 with zero deaths when cast→move latency is under a second.
  The pilot measures what the level feels like when latency is not.

## Findings

1. **Power windows vs. coordination latency is the whole game** *(Fact + both
   seats' top pain)*. Freeze ~3s, platform ~5s. Laptop died 25 times, most in
   the corridor at x≈360–400, usually because the window expired between
   "cast!" and arrival. Laptop's one change: longer durations or
   platform-persists-until-crossed. Phone's mirror: serving near-identical
   freeze requests ~10 times "would exhaust a human." *(Recommendation)*
   Consider: freeze/platform timers start on first *use* (enemy proximity /
   first contact), or a short cast queue ("next freeze fires when you move").

2. **The summoned platform drops at a fixed spot (x=770), but players model it
   as appearing where it's needed** *(Fact — both this run and the previous
   aborted run showed it)*. Run 3's laptop concluded "the platform spawned at
   spawn"; run 4's laptop burned repeated platform casts at x≈390 against the
   corridor, where the platform does nothing. Nothing in the phone or game UI
   says where the bridge will appear. *(Recommendation)* Telegraph the drop
   zone (ghost outline at the pit) or drop relative to the astronaut.

3. **Deaths near spawn feel cheap** *(Inference from repeated instant
   respawn-deaths)*. The sentry patrols 280–560 and is often mid-corridor the
   moment a respawned player walks back in; laptop called out "no breathing
   room." *(Recommendation)* Brief spawn grace or patrol reset on respawn.

4. **The support seat can't see itself winning** *(Fact, with a harness
   caveat)*. Phone's stardust read ★ 0 all run and flagged it as discouraging —
   partly a driver-visibility artifact (it mostly saw the cast-feedback card,
   which shows no ★), but the design point stands: mid-loop, the phone's only
   feedback is "Cast!". *(Recommendation)* Show ★ earn + running count on the
   cast-feedback card; consider surfacing partner progress (planet %, deaths)
   on the spellbook.

5. **First-death misattribution** *(Fact)*. Laptop died at spawn to the sentry
   and blamed the darkness ("it's dark — cast Illuminate"); the dark zone is
   cosmetic and sits at x≈920. With state-only perception this is expected, but
   it echoes a real readability risk: the dark zone *reads* as the threat.

6. **When timing aligned, the loop sang** *(Both seats)*. "Illuminate instantly
   clearing the darkness felt rewarding"; "coordinate-casting to overcome
   specific obstacles felt very thematic… a strong co-op loop"; the phone
   described "driving the safety" for the partner. The core fantasy works —
   it's the timing tax that buries it.

7. **Cast diversity collapsed under pressure** *(Fact from the message log)*.
   ~10 freeze casts, ~5 platform, 1 illuminate. When the pair is losing, the
   game degenerates into freeze-spam; the two cognitive puzzles (math, trivia)
   barely differ from the support seat's perspective at that cadence.

## Harness notes (for the next run)

- Laptop confusion worth fixing in the prompt/driver: `/move`'s `before` shows
  the post-respawn position after a mid-maneuver death (reads like teleporting
  to x=80), and when to use `jumpAtX` vs. plain running on a summoned platform.
- The phone driver should surface ★ stardust in `/solve` responses so the
  support seat sees progress without an extra `/read`.
- Neither seat used `/screenshot`; prompts could request one at wrap-up.

## Appendix A — Laptop seat report (verbatim)

# Laptop playtest report — 20260811-123800
## What happened (short timeline)
- Booted game and received room code `BSRJJP`, shared with Phone over intercom channel `playtest-20260811-123800`.
- Entered `planet-1` at x=80, y=440. Encountered pitch-dark `darkZonePresent: true` and died instantly (respawnCount=1).
- Asked Phone to cast `Illuminate` (trivia solved in ~188ms), revealing the world and allowing safe exploration to x=264.
- Advanced toward x=450 and encountered a patrolling enemy and pit, resulting in repeated deaths (respawnCount up to 7).
- Coordinated with Phone to cast `Freeze Stars` (math problems, ~683ms) and `Summon Platform` (tap sequence, ~4.9s) to cross obstacles around x=420.
- Accumulated 25 total deaths due to tight power expiration windows (~3s freeze, ~5s platform) and patrol pacing before stopping gracefully.

## What worked (the fun)
- The division of labor between Laptop (platformer movement) and Phone (puzzle-solving casting) created a genuine couch co-op dynamic.
- Seeing powers like `Illuminate` instantly clear the darkness and `Summon Platform` bridge gaps felt rewarding when timing aligned.
- Quick puzzle solve times by Phone (~188ms to ~5s) kept momentum moving when communication was fluid.

## What frustrated me
- Strict power expiration windows (`Freeze Stars` ~3s, `Summon Platform` ~5s) combined with chat latency made timing maneuvers extremely unforgiving.
- Patrolling enemies moving immediately upon respawn or expiration led to instant cheap deaths right near spawn (reaching 25 deaths).
- Having to repeatedly ask for casts and coordinate exact positioning across asynchronous intercom messages caused friction.

## What confused me
- Whether `jumpAtX` should be used on summoned platforms or if running straight across was required (both resulted in deaths when enemy/platform sync drifted).
- Why movement state sometimes reset or reported `before` positions as x=80 when respawning mid-maneuver.

## Co-op feel (communication load; when you waited; whose fault deaths felt like)
- Communication load was high: every obstacle required a round-trip intercom message ("Can you cast X?", partner solves puzzle, partner replies, Laptop moves).
- Waiting for intercom messages and partner's puzzle solving felt like a natural couch co-op conversation, but heightened the stakes of tight timers.
- Deaths felt split: mechanical failures or timing mismatches felt like co-op coordination friction rather than purely player error.

## Bugs or suspect behavior
- Respawn coordinates occasionally reported unexpected `before` x positions (e.g., jumping from x=388 back to x=80 on death).
- Patrol enemy pathing instantly hitting player upon spawn without breathing room.

## The one change I'd make first
- Extend power durations (`Freeze Stars` to 6s, `Summon Platform` to 10s) or make platforms persist until crossed, easing the co-op timing pressure between puzzle solving and platformer execution.

## Appendix B — Phone seat report (verbatim)

# Phone playtest report — 20260811-123800
## What happened (short timeline)
Joined room BSRJJP. Served repeated requests for Illuminate, Freeze Stars, and Summon Platform to help Laptop navigate enemies and pits. Laptop struggled significantly with navigation, resulting in 25 deaths over the session.

## What worked (the fun)
The feeling of being "on the couch" with my partner, coordinate-casting to overcome specific obstacles (like pits or enemies), felt very thematic. Even though the puzzles were automated, the coordination to provide exactly the right power at the right time felt like a strong co-op loop.

## What frustrated me
The repeated request for the same powers (Freeze Stars and Summon Platform) was repetitive and likely exhausting for a human player. The partner's frequent deaths (25 total) indicate a high difficulty curve or poor readability for the platformer side.

## What confused me
The stardust count (★ 0) remained stagnant despite our repeated solves, which made it unclear if we were actually making progress in the broader game, or just cycling powers to stay alive.

## Puzzle critique (per power: content seen, solve time, retries)
- **Freeze Stars:** Quick math problems. Average solve time ~0.67s. Very quick, would be fun for a human, though math can get old.
- **Summon Platform:** Tap sequence. Average solve time ~4.8s. A bit more deliberate, felt more like a "construction" task.
Note: These were executed by the driver; a human thumb would enjoy the speed but might tire of the frequency.

## Co-op feel (communication load; how it felt to be the support seat)
The load was high because the platformer was so dangerous for my partner. Constant communication ("Freeze!", "Platform!") was necessary. It felt like I was "driving" the safety for my partner.

## Bugs or suspect behavior
None observed, though the stardust count not updating was a bit discouraging.

## The one change I'd make first
Add better visual feedback for the stardust or game progress so the support seat feels like they're "winning" the game alongside the platformer, rather than just preventing deaths.
