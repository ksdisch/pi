# Planet-1 aim sweep — the experiment pilot 4 owed, 2026-08-13

`PILOT-2026-08-13.md` finding 2 closed on a gap: with the platform fix landed,
planet-1's pit had become an aiming problem, and **no seat in either run ever
issued a valid jump from the pit lip**, so the pilot could not say whether a
correctly-aimed pair clears it. This is that experiment.

It is scripted, not played — `aim-sweep.sh`, no LLM, driven through the same two
drivers the seats use. That is deliberate: both pilot-4 runs died to a free-tier
token-rate 429 inside 5.5 minutes, and this question needs ~70 attempts. A
scripted probe is not a seat, so it may reference the level's geometry.

**68 trials against constellation `604a422`. The answer is not the one the
question expected: the aim is not the variable.**

- **48 bare `jumpAtX` trials — 0 clears.** Every one fell in the pit.
- **19 of them landed on the bridge first, and then ran off its far edge**,
  because nothing released the run.
- **`jumpAtX` + `untilX:780` landed on the bridge 6 for 6**, every time a jump
  actually happened, across a 72px span of take-off points.

So the pit is solvable and the working maneuver is wide open. What the seats are
missing is not accuracy — it is a stop, and any signal that would tell them so.

## Method

One trial = restart the scene (`/planet`, which is a `scene.start('Planet', …)`,
so every trial begins from an identical level with no bridge and the enemy back
at the start of its patrol) → cast Summon Platform for a fresh unarmed bridge →
cast Freeze Stars → one `/move` right from spawn with a `jumpAtX` → settle →
read.

The restart matters, and a smoke run proved why. A bare jump that flies over the
bridge grazes its top face, which **arms** it (`Planet.ts armPlatform` fires from
the collider whenever the astronaut's feet come within 2px of the top), and the
bridge then silently expires five seconds later — mid-flight, in a later trial
that believed it had banked one. Walking home between trials cannot avoid that;
restarting the scene does.

Each trial also carries a **trajectory trace**: `/state` is registered off the
laptop driver's serial command chain, so it answers while a `/move` is still
running, and the script polls it at ~65ms throughout. This is what makes the
result legible. `diedAt` alone cannot separate "bonked the ceiling and dropped in
at x=700" from "flew the whole way, landed on the bridge, and walked off at
x=834" — both drift right while falling and both report the same last-sampled
point. Two early trials at different aims returned *identical* `diedAt` values
for completely different flights.

Geometry, read from constellation's source (the harness never edits that repo):
ground tiles are 64x40 centred at x = 32+64k, skipped for 660 ≤ x < 880, so the
real gap is **640..896** and the astronaut (32x48) falls once its left edge
passes 640 — **x = 656**. The bridge is 96x14 at (770,460): top 453, so standing
on it reads y=429 against y=476 on the ground. y alone therefore says what the
astronaut is standing on, with no arithmetic.

## Block A — bare `jumpAtX`, no `untilX`: 48 trials, 48 deaths

Twelve aim values from 566 to 654, four repetitions each. Not one survived. But
the deaths are three different failures, and only the trace separates them.

| Take-off (measured) | n | Apex y | What happened |
|---|---|---|---|
| 556, 560 | 2 | **394, 395** | head hit the corridor ceiling; fell in short |
| 568 – 612 | 19 | 362–363 | **landed on the bridge**, then ran off it and fell |
| 608 – 644 | 19 | 362–363 | cleared the bridge entirely; fell past it |
| — | 8 | **476** | **never jumped at all** |

**The 19 that landed are the finding.** Touchdown x ran 784–820, dead on the
bridge (it spans 722–818), at a consistent take-off + 204..220. Their aim was
correct. They died anyway, because the move carried `ms:4500` and no `untilX`,
so the astronaut kept holding *right* after touchdown and walked off the far
edge at x≈834.

This is the mechanism behind pilot 4's rule that `untilX` is load-bearing, and it
is not the mechanism that rule was assumed to have. A bare jump does not sail
past the bridge. It lands on the bridge and then leaves.

Two hard walls bound the aim, and neither is visible to a seat:

- **The corridor ceiling** (400x20 at (420,360), spanning x 220–620) caps any
  jump taken much left of x≈564. Apex y comes back 394 instead of 362 — the head
  stops 32px lower — and the astronaut drops in well short of the bridge.
- **`jumpAtX` ≥ 646 produces no jump at all.** All 8 such trials show an apex of
  y=476: the astronaut never left the ground. The driver polls at 60ms, which is
  14px at the 240px/s run speed, so by the time a poll first sees x ≥ 646 the
  astronaut has usually already crossed the fall edge at 656 and is airborne,
  where the jump input does nothing.

  **`/move` still reports `jumped` for every one of them.** That is a telemetry
  lie, and it is exactly the confusion pilot 4 flagged from the other side: run
  B's `jumpAtX: 680` attempt returned `jumped` and `diedAt {772, 592}`,
  identical to its no-jump attempt, and the report could only say the two were
  indistinguishable. They were indistinguishable because they were *the same
  event*. See follow-up 1.

## Block B — `jumpAtX` + `untilX:780`: lands every time a jump happens

Eight trials at four aims. Six landed on the bridge; the two failures were
`jumpAtX: 650`, which never jumped (the wall above), not a miss.

| jumpAtX | Take-off | Result |
|---|---|---|
| 574 | 572, 576 | LAND-platform @788, @788 |
| 598 | 592, 604 | LAND-platform @788, @796 |
| 626 | 620, 644 | LAND-platform @792, @788 |
| 650 | — | no jump — astronaut already past the edge |

Take-offs from **572 to 644** — a 72px span — all converge on the same touchdown,
x 780–796. That is `untilX` doing the work: it ends the move mid-flight, the
driver's `resetInput()` zeroes horizontal input, and `Astronaut.update()` sets
`velocityX` from input every frame including airborne, so the astronaut stops
dead over the cut point and drops straight onto the bridge. The aim barely
matters. The cut is the whole maneuver.

## Block C — park at the lip first, then jump

Pilot 4's run B invented a two-stage plan on its own — park under freeze, then a
separate move to cross — and the report called that the rails solution. It is,
with one qualification the sweep now supplies: **where you park decides whether
you can jump at all.**

| Park (measured) | Cut | n | Result |
|---|---|---|---|
| 624, 628 | none | 2 | jump takes; overshoots the bridge; fell |
| 628 | 780 | 2 | **LAND-platform @796, @780** |
| 636–644 | either | 7 | 5 never jumped; 1 fell; 1 landed |

From a park at 624–628 the jump always takes. From a park at **636 or beyond, 5
of 7 trials never left the ground**: the astronaut crosses the 656 fall edge
inside the first 60ms poll of the next move. Run B parked at x=640 and x=636.
Both of its stage-two attempts were launched from inside the band where a jump
cannot be issued — which is a second, independent reason neither tested the plan.

## What this settles

**Planet-1 does not need a level change.** A working maneuver exists, it is not
pixel-tight, and it survives a 72px error in the one number a seat picks. The
sweep found no evidence for "the jump point is in the wrong place".

**What it needs is feedback**, and the sweep names three specific blind spots —
all observability, none difficulty:

1. A jump that never happened is reported as `jumped` (harness — follow-up 1).
2. Landing on the bridge and then walking off it is indistinguishable from
   missing it: both end `respawned` with a pit `diedAt` (harness — follow-up 2).
3. Nothing tells a seat where the bridge is, so `untilX:780` is not derivable.
   This is the game-UI ask run A's phone seat already made in its own words, and
   pilot 4 recorded as finding 6. The sweep raises its priority: it is not a
   nice-to-have for diagnosis, it is the input to the only parameter that works.

**The round-3 reviewer simulation (F17) is refuted.** It predicted that without
`untilX`, jumps ≤606 would land, 608–634 would die, and 636–648 would clear.
Measured: ≤612 touches the bridge but still dies (0/19 survived); 636–648 does
not clear, and 646+ produces no jump at all. Nothing clears bare, at any value.
It was an unexecuted simulation and it was wrong in the direction that mattered —
it treated the aim as the variable, which is the same assumption the pilot's
seats made.

## Follow-ups this produced

1. **`/move` should report whether the jump was actually taken**, not just that
   the driver pressed the key — the astronaut is airborne past x=656 and the
   input is inert. `jumped` currently means "jump requested". A seat cannot
   diagnose an aim it never got to test, and pilot 4 spent a finding on exactly
   that ambiguity.
2. **`/move` should distinguish "fell from the bridge" from "fell into the pit"**
   — e.g. carry the last surface stood on, or the peak y reached. Nineteen
   trials here were correct jumps that died looking like bad ones.
3. Both are the same shape as `diedAt` in PR #21: the harness already knows the
   answer and throws it away before the seat sees it.
