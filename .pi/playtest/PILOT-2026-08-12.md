# Constellation co-op pilot, run 3 — planet-1, 2026-08-12

Run `20260812-004303`. Seats: laptop `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, phone
`google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`. ~8 minutes of play, **11 deaths, no clear**,
furthest progress **x=400 of a goal at x=920**. The run ended when the laptop
seat was killed by a 429, not by either seat choosing to stop.

This is the first pilot with **armed moves** (`arm:{on:"freeze"|"platform"}`,
merged as `8cd563be9`), which exist to answer one question: with the
cue-to-cast round trip removed, does a coordinated pair clear planet-1?

**It does not, and the reason is now measurable rather than inferred.** All
three armed moves fired correctly and all three still ended in a death. Armed
moves did what they were built to do — they removed latency as the excuse —
and the pair still never reached the pit, let alone the goal.

Runs 1 and 2 are `PILOT-2026-08-11.md` and `PILOT-2026-08-11-run2.md`.

## The first attempt was void — read this before trusting any pilot artifact

Run `20260812-003752` launched first and produced a **phantom clear**: driver
state read `won:true`, `completed:{"planet-1":true}`, planet-2 unlocked. It was
not our pair's clear. It was not a game bug either.

`~/Projects/party-line` contains a **copy of this harness**, and a session there
started its own run (`20260812-003954`) while ours was mid-play. Both default to
driver ports 4801/4802, and `run-pilot.sh` step 4 calls `stop_drivers` before
spawning its own — so each run shut the other's drivers down and took the ports.

The evidence is unambiguous, and none of it is inference:

| Observation | What it proves |
|---|---|
| Our seat's `/screenshot` replied with a path under `party-line/playtest/reports/shots/` | A party-line driver process served our seat's calls on 4801 |
| Our teardown printed `video saved: .../party-line/playtest/reports/video/20260812-003954/` | Our orchestrator shut down *their* drivers |
| Our own videos flushed at 00:38 — mid-run, ~30s long | Our drivers were killed by their `stop_drivers` while our pair was still playing |
| Our seat saw `lastCastPower:"summon-platform"` before our phone had cast anything | We were reading their game (room EESPAC), not ours (room ADRYPB) |
| Their `logs/rails-run2.txt` ends `rails NOT verified` with JSON decode errors | Our teardown broke their run in the same way |

Our pair issued exactly **one** move in that run and **zero** armed moves; the
x=92 → x=892 jump to the win had no move command behind it at all. The run
answers nothing and is not written up further.

*(Fact — harness defect, not a game finding.)* Two checkouts of this harness on
one machine silently corrupt each other and each produces a plausible-looking
result. *(Recommendation)* Derive the default driver ports from the harness path
(or mint them per run) instead of hardcoding 4801/4802, and have `stop_drivers`
refuse to shut down a driver whose `/health` reports a different `HARNESS_DIR`.
The re-run used `LAPTOP_DRIVER_PORT=4811 PHONE_DRIVER_PORT=4812` and was clean —
its videos and screenshots landed in this repo, as they should.

## Did armed moves work?

Mechanically, yes — completely. Three armed moves, three fires, no failures of
the mechanism:

| Time | `arm.on` | `armedForMs` | Events | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 05:43:35 | freeze | 1050 | `arm-fired`, `respawned` | died |
| 05:49:25 | freeze | 864 | `arm-fired`, `respawned` | died |
| 05:49:33 | platform | 4626 | `arm-fired`, `respawned` | died |

**Zero `arm-timeout`. Zero `respawned-while-armed`.** Every arm caught its cast,
and standing armed never once killed the astronaut — the exposure the design doc
worried about (0.9s, 4.6s of standing still) cost nothing on this level.

Outcome-wise, no. 3/3 armed moves ended in a death anyway. The mechanism removed
the ~12s LLM round trip that runs 1 and 2 blamed, and the pair still got no
further than x=400 — worse than run 2's pair, which reached the x≈360–400
corridor by hand. Latency was real, but it was not the wall.

## What the wall actually is

Every pilot so far has had its seats report deaths at coordinates near spawn:
this run's laptop logged "died at x=84 / 88 / 92 / 96" and told its partner so.
**Those are not death sites — they are respawn sites.** After a death, `/move`
returns the state *after* the respawn, so the x a seat reads is where the game
put it back, not where it was killed. The laptop prompt's own advice — "compare
x before/after to learn where the danger was" — reads the wrong number.

Reconstructing from planet-1's config and the measured travel times gives the
real picture. The astronaut runs at 240 px/s from spawn at x=80; the sentry
patrols `corridor.x=420 ± 140`, i.e. **x=280–560**; the pit is x=660–880; the
dark zone and goal are both at x=920.

| Death | Travel before dying | Implied death x |
|---|---|---|
| 05:43:23 | 1239 ms | ≈ 377 |
| 05:43:29 | 1121 ms | ≈ 357 |
| 05:44:13 | 1237 ms | ≈ 377 |
| 05:50:32 | 1605 ms | ≈ 465 |

Every one lands inside the sentry band. **Not one death this run was a pit fall
or anything to do with darkness** — the pair never reached x=660. The sentry,
which starts 200px from spawn, is the entire level as far as three pilots are
concerned.

*(Recommendation, and the highest-value change on this list)* Have the driver
capture x/y at the moment `respawnCount` increments and return it as
`diedAt`, leaving `state` as the post-respawn truth. It is a small driver
change, it costs the game nothing, and it retires a misattribution that has now
distorted the findings of all three pilots.

## Findings

1. **Removing coordination latency did not make planet-1 winnable** *(Fact —
   the arc's question, answered)*. Armed moves fired on 3/3 attempts with no
   timeouts, and every one still ended in a death. Runs 1 and 2 concluded the
   ~3s freeze window could not survive a ~12s chat turn; that was true but
   incomplete. With the chat turn removed, a 3s freeze still does not cover a
   280px-wide patrol band entered from 200px away. *(Recommendation)* The
   design lever is the band, not the timer — a freeze that starts on enemy
   proximity, or a patrol that does not begin within one running second of
   spawn, both fix this where a longer timer only widens the window.

2. **Standing still killed the astronaut twice with no move in flight**
   *(Fact — now measured, not inferred)*. Two deaths landed with no move in
   flight: `respawnCount` went 7→8 and then 8→9 in the gaps *between* driver
   calls, each time resetting the astronaut from x=264 and x=400 back to spawn
   while the seat was composing intercom messages. (The following 9→10 death did
   happen during a move.) Runs 1
   and 2 both asserted "waiting for your partner is lethal"; this is the first
   run where the deaths are visible in the gaps between commands rather than
   attributed after the fact. The co-op loop requires waiting and the level
   punishes it.

3. **A cast was spent on a hazard that was 570px away** *(Fact)*. The laptop's
   first death drew "Can you cast Illuminate?" — the dark zone sits at x=920 and
   the astronaut died around x=377. Run 1's finding #5 called this out; it
   reproduced exactly, because the cause (finding above: seats read respawn x)
   was never fixed. The dark zone *reads* as the threat when you cannot see.

4. **The support seat is blind and says so, again** *(Fact — third run running)*.
   The phone: "The lack of clarity on what exactly was causing the death (enemy,
   pit, or something else) made my support role feel blind," and its one change
   is "more explicit feedback for the Phone player about the state of the world."
   Run 1's finding #4 and run 2's finding #3 are the same ask. Three independent
   phone seats have now volunteered it.

5. **Puzzle solve times are stable across runs and seats** *(Fact — driver
   transcripts)*. Twelve casts, all solved: Illuminate 187ms, Freeze Stars
   672–676ms (n=5), Summon Platform 4835–4869ms (n=5). The ~7× spread run 2
   measured holds to within 35ms across a different session. Summon Platform
   costs ~4.9s to earn and lasts ~5s.

6. **A token-rate 429 killed the laptop seat mid-run** *(Fact — harness/pi,
   not the game)*. The seat died on
   `GenerateContentInputTokensPerModelPerMinute-FreeTier` (250k input
   tokens/min), whose body stated `Please retry in 32.308s` and carried a
   `RetryInfo` of 32s. pi's classifier treats token-rate quotas as fail-fast by
   design — only per-minute *request*-rate quotas were made retryable by the run-2
   fix. A playtest seat resends a growing context every turn, so it walks into
   the input-token cap the longer it plays: the failure mode gets *more* likely
   the better the run is going. *(Recommendation)* Worth revisiting whether a
   token-rate 429 that ships an explicit `RetryInfo` should be retryable on the
   same floored-backoff path as the request-rate case. Flagging, not fixing —
   it is a change to shipped pi retry behavior, not to the harness.

## Honesty caveats

- **Armed moves are driver-reflex.** The seat chose the maneuver, the trigger,
  and when to arm; the driver held it and fired ~200ms after the cast landed.
  That pause is pinned in the driver and not caller-tunable. Progress made under
  an armed move is pre-committed anticipation, not a live read — and in this run
  it produced no progress at all, so nothing here is flattered by it.
- **The driver solved every puzzle**, not the phone player, as in every prior
  run. The phone chose which power and when. Note it reported Illuminate as
  "~4s" in its own write-up where the driver transcript says 187ms — a seat
  estimate, not a measurement.
- **The run is truncated.** ~8 minutes, ended by the 429 rather than by either
  seat judging it done. The pair may have improved with more turns; 11 deaths
  is where it stood when the seat died, not a plateau either seat declared.
- **Deaths are 11 by `respawnCount`**, read from driver state. Run 1 was 25 and
  run 2 was 11, but the three runs had different durations and different
  interventions — the counts are not comparable as a measure of play quality,
  and no trend should be read into them.
- Headless; neither seat saw pixels. The death-site reconstruction above is
  arithmetic from planet-1's config plus measured `elapsedMs`, not observation.
