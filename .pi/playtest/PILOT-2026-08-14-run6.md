Two runs against constellation `604a422` — the first co-op play with finding 7's

arm in the laptop prompt. Seats: laptop `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, phone
`google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`, launched ~26 minutes apart (run A's laptop died
20 minutes before run B began, so run B started on a cold per-minute quota).

Run A `20260814-113951`: ~3m34s of play, 11 deaths (9 captured, 2 invisible),

Run B `20260814-120549`: ~9m05s of play, 10 deaths (10 captured), no clear —

Both laptop seats were killed by the token-rate 429
(`GenerateContentInputTokensPerModelPerMinute-FreeTier`), with `Please retry in

**The question this pilot exists to answer: with the misread fixed, does a
live seat find the release?** Pilot 5 ended with two seats jumping at a
well-reasoned aim and overflying the bridge because nothing cut the input
mid-flight — and its run B misreading a complete death record as "Cleared the
pit". The prep for this pilot taught the reading (finding 7's line); the
experiment was whether a seat that now *reads* its overflights correctly goes
on to issue the release that works (`untilX` over the bridge — the aim
sweep's 6-for-6 pairing, never yet issued live).

**Half the answer arrived: the reading is fixed, the release is still
unfound.** Run B's laptop took off three times — `tookOff: true, apexY: 362`
at pressed x=652, 644, 640 — and all three arcs came down 74–78px past the
bridge's far end (`diedAt` x=892–896), every one fully captured because every
one was `ms`-bounded. The seat read all three correctly: "took off
successfully (apexY 362), but still fell into pit past x=648" — no "cleared
the pit", no distance-as-progress on any *captured* death. (Run A broadcast
one misread on a death the driver never recorded — finding 1 carries the
scope.) But the seat then spent its remaining life interrogating the
*platform* ("Where does Summon Platform place the platform?"), never the
input: it varied `jumpAtX` by 12px, re-cast platforms, and never once
shortened the run. Its terminators were `ms: 4000` throughout. Run A even
brushed the right range — a walk-in at `untilX: 800`, inside the bridge
span, and another at `untilX: 720`, just short of it (the sweep's one
validated cut is 780) — but never while airborne; its only jump used
`untilX: 1100`. No seat has ever issued a bridge-band `untilX` *together
with* a `jumpAtX`, in six pilots.

## The release experiment, run by run

Run A's laptop never got a take-off. Its one `jumpAtX` (aim 645, with
`untilX: 1100`) pressed at x=656 — the fall edge itself — and reported
`jump-ignored, tookOff: false, apexY: 476`: the aim was never tested (the
~14px/poll sampling the DESIGN warns about, reproduced live). Every other pit
approach was a walk-in at ground level with the platform standing overhead
(platformCount 1 from 16:41:44 on), dying at `diedAt` x=768–780, y=585–608
with `lastStoodAt` at the lip (644–656, y=476), five times. One of those
walk-ins carried `untilX: 800` (16:42:05) — inside the bridge span 722–818,
though the only cut the sweep ever validated is 780; a further walk-in at
`untilX: 720` (16:42:16), its cut 2px short of the span, is the uncaptured
death finding 1 discusses. So the release's value space has been brushed;
no move has ever paired a bridge-band `untilX` with a `jumpAtX`, which is
the whole trick.

Run B's laptop produced the pilot's data. All three take-offs, all
freeze-armed except the third:

- 17:07:33.156 `{arm: freeze, ms: 4000, jumpAtX: 648}` → `arm-fired, jumped,
  respawned`, `jump: {tookOff: true, pressedAt: {652, 476}, apexY: 362}`,
  `diedAt {892, 592}`, `lastStoodAt {652, 476}`.
- 17:13:53.515 `{arm: freeze, ms: 4000, jumpAtX: 640}` → the same shape:
  `pressedAt {644, 476}, apexY: 362`, `diedAt {892, 592}`,
  `lastStoodAt {644, 476}`.
- 17:15:17.726 `{arm: platform, ms: 4000, jumpAtX: 640}` → instant fire
  (`armedForMs` 61, a platform already standing), `pressedAt {640, 476}`,
  `diedAt {896, 602}`, `lastStoodAt {640, 476}`.

Three trials, one aim band (640–652), one apex (362), one outcome: overflew
the whole bridge, came down past its far end, `lastStoodAt` pinned at the
takeoff point — never stood on anything past it. The aim sweep's headline,
now reproduced five times from the seat side across two pilots (pilot 5's
two trials plus these three), and the seat's own reading agreed each time.

What the seat did next is the finding. It had the correct diagnosis in hand
— "took off, fell past x=648" — and reached for every lever except the one
that matters: it asked where platforms spawn, asked its partner about cast
timing, moved its aim 648 → 652 → 640, parked safely at x=564 under a freeze
(the two-stage plan pilot 4 invented) and asked for a fresh platform "so it
spawns at x=564 where I am". The horizontal input that carried it over the
bridge was never suspected. `ms: 4000` outlives the whole flight, so the run
held *right* from press to death in every trial, and nothing in the prompt
connects "the run keeps going while the input is held" to "a jump's landing
is chosen by where the run stops". The prompt teaches `jumpAtX` as "the way
a human takes a gap"; it teaches `untilX` only as "stop at that x". The
seats have now proven those two lines don't compose on their own.

## The intercom boundary bug — two clean instances, one signature

Pilot 5's finding 8 was one unexplained 20.8s delivery to an idle seat. This
pilot produced two instances of something sharper, with a signature exact
enough to script:

**A message created ~1s before an `intercom_wait` begins is invisible to
that wait.** The wait runs to its full timeout deaf; the message is
delivered only as the wait ends.

- Run A: the laptop asked "cast Freeze Stars? arming on freeze" at
  16:42:22.815 and armed 1.03s later — protocol followed, same turn. The
  phone's `intercom_wait` began at 16:42:23.878, **1.06s after the message
  existed**, and did not receive it. The wait sat 92.8s until the laptop's
  *next* message (16:43:56.243) ended it — and the two messages arrived out
  of order, the newer one first. The phone never cast; the laptop's arm
  stood the full 90s and reported `arm-timeout`. Pilot 5 eliminated the
  deadlock class by fixing seat behaviour; this 90s stall was manufactured
  by the harness with both seats behaving correctly.
- Run B: the laptop's "arming on freeze with jumpAtX: 652" was created at
  17:11:01.077; the phone's wait began at 17:11:02.100, **1.02s later**, ran
  its full 60s, timed out at 17:12:02.102 — and the message was delivered
  2ms after the timeout. The freeze arrived 63s after the ask; the arm
  survived it (`armedForMs` 63476) only because a 90s ceiling happens to
  exceed a 60s wait.
- The control, same run: a message created ~25s *into* a wait (17:13:45.196,
  wait started 17:13:20.322) was delivered in 0.2s. Deliveries into a
  running wait are fine; the failure window is specifically a message that
  lands in the gap before the wait starts.

Two further latency shapes, consistent with pilot 5's: an 18.6s delivery to
a seat idle *without* a wait (created 17:07:42.342, delivered 17:08:00.990 —
finding 8's shape), and a 105.7s delivery into a seat holding a 90s armed
`/move` (created 17:07:46.795, delivered 17:09:32.447, exactly when the curl
returned — steer deliveries land at turn boundaries, so a long armed hold is
a long deaf spell by construction).

This folds directly into BACKLOG item 9, which now has a deterministic
recipe: send, then start a wait ~1s later, and measure. The two instances
cost these runs ~93s and ~61s respectively.

## The 429 wall — and run B's 9 minutes

Run A repeated pilot 5's pattern: 45 assistant turns in 3m34s (12.6/min,
summed `input + cacheRead` 693k), killed mid-plan. Run B is the outlier that
proves the mechanism: 58 turns in 9m05s (6.4/min, 966k) — the slowest
cadence and the longest life of any pilot seat, because ~4 minutes of it
were spent inside two 90s arm-timeouts and a 63s armed hold, none of which
cost tokens. Pilot 4's finding ("the deadlocks were the only thing that kept
either run alive") reproduced under lab conditions: the harness's stalls are
the free tier's relief valve. The 20-minute cold gap before run B also
helped its first minutes, per pilot 5's spacing lesson. The token-rate-429
retryability item (BACKLOG Active — referenced by title) remains the unblock
for a pilot that is both long *and* fluent.

## Findings

1. **Finding 7's teaching worked on every captured death — and the misread
   survived exactly where there is no record** *(Fact — the prep's question,
   answered with a scope)*. Three fully-captured overflight deaths, three
   correct readings ("took off successfully, but still fell into pit past
   x=648"), zero "cleared the pit". The one-line rule — `lastStoodAt` at or
   before the takeoff point means you never landed — was applied as written.
   The exception is run A's: after the `reached-x` that ended at x=724,
   y=519 mid-fall (an invisible death, no `diedAt`, no `lastStoodAt`), the
   seat broadcast "Got across the pit to x=724, but died" — the textbook
   misread, intact, because the rule is conditioned on a `lastStoodAt` the
   seat did not have. The teaching covers captured deaths only; the
   no-record path still licenses distance-as-progress, which is a concrete
   argument for BACKLOG item 8's idle sampler.

2. **Correct reading did not produce the correct release — the input model
   is the missing piece, and it needs teaching** *(Fact + Recommendation —
   prompts, next pilot)*. Five live overflights across two pilots, and no
   seat has suspected the held input; this run's seat interrogated platform
   placement instead, three times. The prompt's `jumpAtX` and `untilX` lines
   don't compose into "your landing is chosen by where the run stops".
   One technique line would close it: "a jump carries your run with it — you
   keep moving sideways in the air for as long as the move holds `right`, so
   to land ON something, set `untilX` a little past where you want to come
   down; an `untilX` (or `ms`) far beyond it means you fly over everything."
   Game-agnostic, no level coordinates, same register as the existing
   `jumpAtX` teaching.

3. **The intercom wait-start boundary drops messages — now with a
   deterministic signature** *(Fact — harness, two instances)*. A message
   created ~1s before an `intercom_wait` begins is not delivered into it;
   the wait runs deaf to timeout, and delivery can be out of order. Cost
   here: one manufactured 90s arm-timeout (both seats correct) and one 63s
   armed hold. Folds into BACKLOG item 9 with a scriptable repro: send,
   wait 1s, `intercom_wait`, measure.

4. **The arm-timeout class returned, split three ways** *(Fact)*. One
   harness-caused (finding 3), two seat drifts — the ask-without-arm shape
   pilot 5 saw once, and a new one: arming on a freeze the seat had just
   observed to be spent, without asking fresh. F6's conditional teaches the
   arm-when-frozen half; the arm-when-*not*-frozen half ("your partner's
   'cast' message is not a live window — check `/state`, and if it's false,
   ask fresh in the same message that says you're arming") is the half both
   drifts fell through.

5. **Both invisible deaths were run A's, and run B's zero is luck, not
   progress** *(Fact — consistent with BACKLOG item 8)*. 19 of 21 captured;
   the 2 misses are the between-moves class, one of them again the
   `reached-x`-ended-mid-fall shape. Whether a death is visible still
   depends on the terminator the seat happened to pick.

6. **The handoff digest injection reproduced in all four seats** *(Fact —
   BACKLOG item 10)*. Run A's seats opened on pilot 5's phone-session
   digest; run B's on run A's. Each laptop burned its first turn reasoning
   about the role conflict ("The handoff says I'm 'Phone,' but my core role
   is 'Laptop'"). The contamination is now a chain, run to run to run.

7. **Neither phone honored the stop rule, and no seat report was written in
   either run** *(Fact)*. Run A's phone did three 300s waits and a glance
   probe, then was ceiling-killed mid-wait; run B's did *fourteen*
   consecutive ~60s waits (rule: wrap after three) before the ceiling. Both
   laptops died on the 429 before their report step. Four seats, zero
   reports — everything in this document is derived from transcripts, notes
   files, and intercom traffic. The stop-rule finding from pilot 5
   (run B) is now 3-for-4 against.

## Honesty caveats

- **Armed moves are driver-reflex.** Every patrol-band crossing rode an
  armed move; label all such progress accordingly. The three take-offs were
  freeze-armed (two) and platform-instant (one).
- **The driver solved every puzzle** (freeze ~0.67s, platform ~4.9s,
  illuminate ~0.2s — consistent with pilots 3–5).
- **The couch glance is a harness affordance**; both phones used `/read`'s
  `world`/`worldLastDeath` for their situation reports.
- **No seat wrote a report in either run** (finding 7 above); laptop-side
  claims come from notes files, intercom traffic, and the session
  transcripts; phone-side likewise.
- Death counts are not a quality metric across runs of different lengths.
- The release analysis rests on three take-offs at one aim band, all
  `ms`-terminated; no live jump has yet carried a bridge-band `untilX`.
  `diedAt`'s known biases apply to every table row;
  `reports/video/20260814-113951/laptop.webm` and
  `reports/video/20260814-120549/laptop.webm` are the way to falsify the
  overflight readings.
