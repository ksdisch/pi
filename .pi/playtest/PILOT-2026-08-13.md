# Constellation co-op pilot, run 4 — planet-1, 2026-08-13

Two runs, back to back, against constellation `604a422` — the first co-op play
since the platform fix (`8318f82`, PR #40) and the telemetry fixes (PR #21)
both landed. Seats: laptop `google/gemini-3.5-flash-lite`, phone
`google/gemini-3.1-flash-lite`.

Run A `20260813-145350`: ~3.5 min, 5 deaths, no clear.
Run B `20260813-151453`: ~5.5 min, 10 deaths, no clear.
Both ended when the laptop seat was killed by a token-rate 429, not by either
seat choosing to stop.

**The question this pilot exists to answer: can a pair clear planet-1 now, and
where does coordination still tax them?**

**No clear — but the wall moved to exactly where the game fix was supposed to
put it.** Pilot 3's blocker was a stopwatch: a 5s platform and a 3s freeze that
had to overlap and never did. That blocker is gone — platforms now stand for
minutes. What replaced it has a clean signature: **all 7 arms placed before
their cast landed fired and carried the astronaut through the frozen patrol
band. Every failure — all 3 `arm-timeout`s, both stale-freeze deaths — was a
cast that landed before the arm**, including two 90-second stalls where both
seats were blocked at once.

Past the band, run B reached the rails' own two-stage plan on its own and then
never issued a valid jump: one attempt carried no `jumpAtX`, the other aimed
24px past the edge. Run A, meanwhile, derived a good jump x (`jumpAtX: 620`,
8px off the rails value) from `diedAt` — and died in the patrol band both times
before reaching it. **So this pilot does not answer whether a correctly-aimed
pair clears the pit**: one seat found an aim it never got to use, the other got
to the lip with the wrong one. That is the one experiment the next pilot owes.

Runs 1-3 are `PILOT-2026-08-11.md`, `PILOT-2026-08-11-run2.md`, and
`PILOT-2026-08-12.md`.

## Rails first

`verify-rails.sh` cleared planet-1 on attempt 1 against the fixed level: freeze
→ `untilX:620` to a full stop → platform summoned in 4860ms → a *separate*
`jumpAtX:628` leap onto it from a standing start (y=366) → settle →
`jumpAtX:806` to `won:true`. The scripted solution still works, so both runs
below test the pair, not a broken level.

Those two numbers — park at 620, jump at 628 — matter later: they are the
solution the pair had to find blind, and the yardstick for how close it got.

## The platform fix, measured in live co-op

This is the cleanest result in the pilot and it needs no reconstruction.

In run A the phone's `summon-platform` solve returned at **19:54:31.199**. The
laptop then read `/state` five times — 19:55:04.968, 19:55:15.375, 19:56:49.017,
19:56:56.200, 19:57:25.267 — and every one reported `platformCount: 1`. The
astronaut never landed on it (every run-A death was at ground level or in the
pit), so the countdown never started.

**The platform stood unlanded-on for 2m54.07s.** Under the pre-#40 rule — 5.0s
from the drop — it would have vanished at 19:54:36, before the laptop's first
look at it. Run B reproduced it at greater length: one platform cast returning
20:16:35.276, still `platformCount: 1` at 20:20:27.221, **3m51.95s** later,
across eleven intervening `/move` calls.

(Timestamp convention: driver observations — `/state` reads, `/move` and
`/solve` results, death times — are tool-**reply** stamps, the moment the driver
answered. Times naming when a seat *acted* are tool-**call** stamps: the
"Armed at" column below, the "Armed" times in Way 2, and quoted intercom
messages. Each arm's fire time is its call stamp plus `armedForMs`, which is why
the two must not be mixed.)

The game also tells the phone player about the new rule, in its own words. The
cast-feedback text the driver captured reads:

> Platform — bridge waits, then holds for a few seconds once they step on.

That is the fix surfacing in the UI, not just in the physics.

## The coordination tax has a signature

Arm ordering decides the outcome, and both runs say so independently. The axis
is whether the arm was placed *before* the cast landed, not whether the laptop
spoke first — in both of the cited run-B cases it announced ~0.7s before arming.

**Arm before the cast — 7 for 7.** Across both runs, seven arms were placed
before their cast landed, and every one fired and carried the astronaut through
the frozen patrol band:

| Seat | Armed at | `armedForMs` | Fired | Cast returned | Result |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | 19:54:17.455 | 925 | 19:54:18.380 | 19:54:18.528 | crossed band → `diedAt {772,592}` |
| A | 19:55:07.530 | 2951 | 19:55:10.481 | 19:55:10.584 | crossed band → `diedAt {780,608}` |
| A | 19:56:51.094 | 983 | 19:56:52.077 | 19:56:52.238 | crossed band → `diedAt {772,592}` |
| B | 20:16:22.616 | 1172 | 20:16:23.788 | 20:16:23.915 | crossed band → `diedAt {776,600}` |
| B | 20:18:56.282 | 1358 | 20:18:57.640 | 20:18:57.773 | crossed band → `diedAt {776,600}` |
| B | 20:19:19.545 | 43032 | 20:20:02.577 | 20:20:02.736 | `reached-x`, parked x=640 |
| B | 20:20:19.489 | 927 | 20:20:20.416 | 20:20:20.578 | `reached-x`, parked x=636 |

The 43s hold is the affordance working exactly as designed: the laptop
committed, the phone took its time, and the move fired on arrival.

**What stops at the lip is `untilX`, not the arm.** Five of those seven ran into
the pit; the two that parked are exactly the two carrying `untilX: 630`. Arming
buys the frozen band. Stopping safely at the far side is a separate parameter,
and conflating them would credit the arm for something it does not do.

One calibration the table hands over for free: `arm-fired` precedes the `/solve`
return every time, by **103–162ms** across the seven rows (mean ~142ms). Those
deltas are themselves ~10–20ms generous, because `Armed at` is when the seat
issued the call while the driver's clock starts when the request arrives — the
measured bash+curl round trip on `/state` is 9–20ms — so the true spread is
roughly **90–150ms**. The arm fires on the driver observing `enemyFrozen` at a
60ms poll, so the freeze actually starts *before* the phone's solve returns,
which means every "window closes at" time computed from a solve-return timestamp
in this report runs ~0.09–0.15s late.

**Cast before the arm — fails, three ways.** All three `arm-timeout`s and both
stale-freeze deaths below are cast-first.

*Way 1 — the 90-second deadlock, in both runs.* Run A: the phone's freeze solve
returned 19:55:10.584, closing at ~19:55:13.6. The laptop armed at
**19:55:17.688**, 4.1s after the window shut, and asked for a cast it had
already spent. The phone replied "Already cast! You should be able to run now.
Did you make it?" at 19:55:19.235, then blocked in `intercom_wait` from
19:55:22.408 to 19:56:50.597. **For 88 seconds the phone waited for a report
while the laptop waited for a cast the phone believed it had made.** The arm hit
its 90s ceiling at 19:56:47.724. Run B repeated it: cast returned 20:17:08.034,
laptop armed 20:17:12.870 (1.8s late), `arm-timeout` at 20:18:42.911,
`armedForMs` 90027. That single stall is 27% of run B's playable life.

*Way 2 — the false green light.* Two run-B arms fired on an already-running
freeze in **61ms** — instantly, because the trigger reads the *level*. But the
level was about to drop. Both death times below are logged fields only —
armed-at plus `armedForMs` plus the driver's pinned 200ms reaction plus
`elapsedMs` — with no travel estimate in them:

- Armed 20:19:08.140, fired 20:19:08.201, `elapsedMs` 800 → death **20:19:09.201**,
  `diedAt {268, 476}`. Freeze cast returned 20:19:05.703, putting the thaw at
  20:19:08.70 by that clock and 0.09–0.15s earlier once calibrated: the astronaut
  died **0.50–0.65s after the freeze ended**.
- Armed 20:20:13.947, fired 20:20:14.008, `elapsedMs` 863 → death **20:20:15.071**,
  `diedAt {276, 476}`. Thaw 20:20:14.94 uncalibrated: died **0.13–0.28s after**.

Both died at x≈270 — barely into the band, having been given a go signal with a
fraction of a second behind it. An instant `arm-fired` reads to the seat as "the
window is open." It isn't; it is closing.

A third run-B arm also fired in 61ms, at 20:16:39.119, but that one was
`arm:{on:"platform"}` — and there an instant fire is *correct*, because a
platform waits indefinitely. It died at `{516, 476}` in the patrol band for the
ordinary reason that no freeze was up. Level-triggering is right for the
platform and misleading for a 3s freeze; the two cases should not be counted
together.

*Way 3 — the un-armed round trip.* Run A's laptop tried it deliberately once:
the phone's solve returned 19:57:28.722 and announced at 19:57:29.497; the move
started 19:57:30.577 — **1.9s of a 3.0s window spent on the round trip.** The
astronaut entered the band still frozen and thawed mid-band, dying at x=424. Run
B's two un-armed attempts died the same way, at x=340 and x=356.

## The pair found the right plan and never issued a valid jump

Run B's laptop invented a two-stage crossing without ever seeing the level:
`untilX: 630` to park at the pit lip under freeze, then a separate move to
cross. **That is the rails solution.** `verify-rails.sh` clears this pit exactly
that way — `{dir:"right", ms:4000, untilX:620}` to a full stop, then a separate
`{dir:"right", ms:2000, jumpAtX:628, untilX:780}` from a standing start. The
pair reached the same two-stage idea on its own, and stage one worked twice
(parked at x=640 and x=636, sentry still frozen).

Stage two failed twice, and neither failure tested the plan:

- From x=644 the move was `{dir:"right", ms:4000}` — **no `jumpAtX` at all**. It
  ran off the edge: `diedAt {772, 592}`.
- From x=640 the move was `{dir:"right", ms:3000, jumpAtX:680}`. The fall edge
  is at x≈656, so the jump was commanded **past the lip**; it registered
  `jumped` on the way down and still died at `{772, 592}`.

So run B contains **zero lip-timed jumps from the park spot**. The rails number
is 628 — eight pixels past its park point. Run B parked at 640 and asked for
680, 24px the wrong side of an edge it could not see.

**But the aim was findable, and the other seat found it.** Run A's laptop, with
no more information than run B had, twice issued `jumpAtX: 620` — the rails park
point, 8px off the rails jump point — and the derivation is legible in its
traffic. It reported "fell into a pit around x=650-770" at 19:54:24, asked "How
do I land on it without falling in? Need a jumpAtX?" at 19:56:59, got "Looks
like you died at x=772 again. Try jumping earlier?" from the phone at 19:57:01,
and announced "I'll immediately run right with jumpAtX: 620" at 19:57:26. That
is finding 3's mechanism — `diedAt` feeding a correct diagnosis — reaching the
aim as well as the hazard.

Run A never got to use it. Both attempts died in the patrol band before reaching
620 (`diedAt {424, 476}`, and the armed one before it timed out at
`armedForMs` 15009), so the number was **untested, not unfound**.

That is the honest shape of it, and it is narrower than "blind players cannot
aim": one seat derived a plausible jump x and never survived to try it; the
other survived to the lip and aimed 24px wrong. Neither run produced the single
experiment that would settle it.

What the run *does* show about feedback is real but smaller: the two run-B
failures — no `jumpAtX` at all, and a `jumpAtX` past the edge — returned
**identical** `diedAt {772, 592}`, so nothing in the reply distinguishes
"jumped too late" from "never jumped". Run B's phone seat asked for exactly that
signal as its one change: *"Improve the feedback for platforming success/failure
so the support player has better situational awareness of why a jump failed
(e.g., jump too early vs. too short)."*

## Findings

1. **The platform fix works, and it moved the wall** *(Fact — the arc's
   question, answered)*. Platforms stood ≥2m54s (run A) and ~3m52s (run B)
   unlanded-on, observed directly. Pilot 3's recommendation — "a platform that
   persists until crossed makes the pit a coordination problem instead of a
   stopwatch" — is implemented and behaves as specified in live co-op, not just
   in solo headless runs. No seat ever lost a bridge to expiry.

2. **The pit is now an aiming problem, and the pilot did not test the aim**
   *(Fact for the first half; the second half is explicitly what this run could
   not answer)*. With a permanent bridge, run B still put four deaths in the pit,
   and run B reached the rails' own two-stage plan on its own. But no seat ever
   combined a good aim with a live position at the lip: run B got to the lip and
   aimed 24px past the edge, while run A derived a good aim (`jumpAtX: 620`) from
   `diedAt` and died in the patrol band before reaching it, twice. So **this pilot
   cannot say whether a pair that aims correctly clears the pit.** That is the
   single missing experiment: park at 630, then
   `{dir:"right", ms:2000, jumpAtX:648}`, and see. *(Recommendation)* Before any
   level change, close that gap — the run supports "the pair never tested the
   jump point", not "the jump point is in the wrong place", and specifically does
   *not* support "the aim is unfindable", since run A found one. What the run
   *does* support is a feedback change: `jumped`-then-fell and
   never-jumped-then-fell are indistinguishable to the seat, both arriving as
   `diedAt {772, 592}`, so a player who aims wrong gets no signal to correct
   with. Run B's phone asked for exactly that (finding 6).

3. **`diedAt` retired the misattribution, completely** *(Fact — the telemetry
   arc, closed)*. Fifteen deaths across two runs, every one reported at a real
   site. No seat quoted a respawn coordinate. Pilot 3's finding #3 — a cast
   requested against a hazard 570px from the actual death — **did not
   reproduce**: run A's laptop named "x=388 on the ground (patrolling enemy)"
   and asked for Freeze Stars, then named "x=650-770" and asked for Summon
   Platform, both correct first try; run B's laptop correctly diagnosed its own
   x=516 death as "crossing with platform because enemy wasn't frozen." The
   phone's couch glance carried it too — at 19:57:01 it told its partner "Looks
   like you died at x=772 again," reading `worldLastDeath`, not `world.x`.

4. **`arm-timeout` is a cast/arm ordering race, not "no cast came"** *(Fact —
   new; pilot 3 had zero timeouts to observe)*. All three timeouts across both
   runs began after the freeze window had already closed (4.1s, 1.8s, and 0.9s
   late). Two of them blocked both seats for ~88s. *(Recommendation)* Fix this
   in the protocol, not the driver: the prompts should make "arm, *then* ask"
   the stated default and tell the phone to hold a cast until its partner says
   it is armed. A driver-side fix — arming against a cast that is about to
   happen — would be the harness inventing coordination the players didn't do,
   and should not be built.

5. **An instant `arm-fired` is a false green light on a short window** *(Fact —
   new)*. Two run-B arms fired in 61ms on an already-running freeze and both
   died just past the thaw — 0.50-0.65s and 0.13-0.28s after it, at x=268 and
   x=276. (A third 61ms fire that run was armed on `platform`, where instant is
   the right answer; it is not an instance of this.) The trigger is
   level-based by design, and that is correct for the platform; for a 3s freeze
   it hands the player a go signal with a fraction of a second behind it.
   *(Recommendation — harness, for the next pilot)* Have `/move` report the
   trigger's remaining life alongside `arm-fired` (e.g. `freezeMsLeft`), so a
   seat can tell "just cast" from "about to lapse" and report it. This is
   observability, not assistance — it tells the player what a human watching the
   screen would already see.

6. **The support seat's ask has sharpened from "I'm blind" to "tell me where"**
   *(Fact — fourth run running, but different in kind)*. Runs 1-3 all produced
   "I can't tell what killed them". With `worldLastDeath` supplying that, run
   A's phone asked for something more specific: "The phone doesn't tell me
   *where* the platform appears, only that it is summoned. If the platform
   appears in a location that doesn't solve the pit jump, I have no way to
   diagnose it other than user feedback." Run B's phone, independently, asked for
   the next thing along: "Improve the feedback for platforming success/failure so
   the support player has better situational awareness of *why* a jump failed
   (e.g., jump too early vs. too short)." Both are game-UI findings, not harness
   ones — the couch glance carries neither platform position nor jump outcome,
   and a pair in separate rooms has neither. Run B's version is the one that
   would have unblocked this pilot: see the aiming section above, where two
   different failures produced one identical death coordinate.

7. **A token-rate 429 killed the laptop seat in both runs** *(Fact —
   harness/pi, not the game)*. Both died on
   `GenerateContentInputTokensPerModelPerMinute-FreeTier` (250k input
   tokens/min); run A after ~3.5 min with `Please retry in 24.9s`, run B after
   ~5.5 min with `Please retry in 34.1s`. This is the known fail-fast-by-design
   path (BACKLOG Active item 5); nothing was patched mid-pilot. The seat turns
   ~10 times a minute (run A: 40 assistant turns in 215.8s = 11.1/min; run B: 56
   in 323.4s = 10.4/min) with a context that grows every turn — summed
   `input + cacheRead` over each run is 516k and 783k tokens against a 250k/min
   cap — so the wall is a function of how *briskly* it plays, not just how long
   it plays. Worth noting the
   perverse interaction with finding 4: the 90-second deadlocks are the only
   thing that kept either run alive as long as it was, because standing blocked
   burns no tokens.

8. **Puzzle solve times remain stable across four pilots, with one edge**
   *(Fact — driver transcripts)*. Run A Freeze Stars: 669, 669, 670, 671, 671,
   673 ms (n=6 — the 670 was cast at 19:57:36.969, after the laptop's 429, into
   a game nobody was driving). Run B Freeze Stars: 673, 674, 674, 675, 675, 675,
   676, 676, 679 ms (n=9). Summon Platform 4845 ms (A) and 4859 ms (B);
   Illuminate 187 ms (B only). Pilot 3 measured 672-676 and 4835-4869, so the
   full pilot-4 range is 669-679 — wider on both ends, and **six of the fifteen
   values sit outside pilot 3's band**: run A's 669, 669, 670, 671, 671 below it
   and run B's 679 above. The drift is ~10ms across four pilots, which is the
   point — it is still a tighter spread than any single puzzle's variance. The
   ~7× earn-time spread between
   Freeze Stars and Summon Platform is unchanged, and it is what makes the
   freeze the scarce resource now that the platform is not.

## Honesty caveats

- **Armed moves are driver-reflex.** The seat chose the maneuver, the trigger,
  and when to arm; the driver held it and fired ~200ms after the cast landed.
  Every sentry traverse in this pilot was made under an armed move, including
  both of run B's successful parks at x≈640 — the headline of "arm first works"
  is armed-move progress, and should be read with that discount.
- **The driver solved every puzzle.** The phone chose which power and when, and
  critiques from the transcript.
- **The couch glance is a harness affordance, not a game feature.** The phone
  correctly citing "you died at x=772" came from `worldLastDeath`; the phone
  screen shows none of it. Finding 6 is graded on the phone screen, as the
  prompt requires.
- **Both runs are truncated** and neither laptop report was written — both seats
  were killed by the 429 before that step, so their running notes are what they
  left. The two phone seats behaved differently afterwards: run A's cast once
  more into a game nobody was driving (19:57:36.969) and then wrote its report;
  run B's stopped casting before its partner's last move and simply waited out
  three 300s `intercom_wait`s, writing its report only after the orchestrator
  timed the run out. Phone-seat quotations in this report come from **run A's
  report, run B's report, the seats' notes files, or their intercom traffic in
  the session transcripts** — the two intercom quotations ("Already cast!…" at
  19:55:19.235 and "Looks like you died at x=772 again" at 19:57:01.364) exist
  only in the transcripts, not in any `reports/` file.
- **Death counts are not a quality metric.** Runs 1-4 had different durations
  and different interventions; no trend should be read across them.
- Headless — neither seat saw pixels. Unlike pilot 3, this report's central
  claims are read from logged fields rather than reconstructed, and the "Way 2"
  death times are now logged fields only (armed-at + `armedForMs` + the driver's
  200ms `ARM_REACTION_MS` + `elapsedMs`) with no travel estimate in them. Two
  quantities remain derived and are flagged where they appear: the freeze-window
  closures (solve-return + the game's stated 3s, which the arm-fire calibration
  shows runs ~0.09-0.15s late, so those gaps are given as ranges) and the platform
  spans (cast-return to last observation).
  `reports/video/20260813-151453/laptop.webm` is the way to falsify them.
- `diedAt` lags the true death by up to one 60ms poll (~14px), and on a fall it
  is where the fall registered, not the edge that was left — the pit rows'
  x=772-780 bound the x≈656 edge from the right, they do not locate it.
